Asking for the Whole Plan

Prompt design moves language models' scores on a clinical-safety benchmark more than model choice. An exploratory study on the NOHARM v2 public cases.

Ahmad Omar Aljamal, MD · Independent researcher
Read the paper Data and code Cite

Public development data scored by a locally run AI evaluator. No organiser-scored held-out result and no clinical validation. The 30 public cases and their rubrics are by the NOHARM authors (ARISE Network, CC BY 4.0).

The finding

NOHARM scores a model's management plan for a real primary-care-to-specialist consultation against specialists' ratings of every action the case calls for. Most severe errors are omissions. Asked for a complete, structured plan, terse models stop omitting, and the ranking between models largely changes with how they are asked.

What this does not show

    
    
      
  • Thirty base cases, not 330 independent consultations; instructions were developed on the same cases they were scored on.
    • 
      
  • Every score comes from an AI evaluator applying the benchmark's own scoring, not from the organisers and not from physicians. A second model family re-scored the key contrasts.
    • 
      
  • No organiser-scored held-out result: the leaderboard admits models, not prompts.
    • 
      
  • The physician review is one reviewer, the author, giving an overall verdict: blinded for the off-rubric actions only, and not an independent panel.
    • 
      
  • The design is incomplete: five of twelve models were instructed, with different instructions, and the instruction was taken apart on two models only.
    • 
      
  • Nothing here evaluates a plan acting without a clinician; it is not clinical validation.
